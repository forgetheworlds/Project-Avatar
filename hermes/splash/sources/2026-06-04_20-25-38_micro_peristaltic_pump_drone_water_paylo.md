Key Technical Trade-Offs for Drone Payload Integration 

When configuring a sub-20g pump for an aerial water payload, certain physics-based constraints must be managed: 

* **Flow Rate vs. Weight**: Sub-20g peristaltic pumps max out at roughly **1 to 3 mL/min**. If your drone project requires rapid water misting or higher delivery volume, you must scale up to slightly heavier micro-pumps (such as the 115g [Takasago RP-2GⅡ Series](https://www.takasago-fluidics.com/collections/micro-peristaltic-pump-rp-2g%E2%85%B1-series) at 25–80 mL/min) or pivot to a 5W diaphragm pump system. 
* **Suction Lift Limits**: Like all vacuum-generating displacement hardware, micro-peristaltic mechanisms face a maximum environmental suction lift of roughly 10 meters at sea level. Tubes dropped from high-hovering drones must factor in this limitation. 

* **Alternative Technologies (Piezoelectric)**: If weight is your ultimate constraint and continuous rotary movement is not required, consider a **Piezoelectric Micropump** (such as the
  Bartels Mikrotechnik BP7
). Piezoelectric options offer rapid dynamic response and lower power draw in a sub-10g footprint, though they lack the positive-displacement hose containment of a true peristaltic pump.
