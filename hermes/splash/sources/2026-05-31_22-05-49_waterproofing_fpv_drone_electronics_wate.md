1. Prepare Workspace and Electronics 

* **Work ventilation**: Work in a well-ventilated area or outdoors because silicone coatings release strong fumes during application.
* **Clean surfaces**: Clean your flight controller (FC) and electronic speed controller (ESC) thoroughly using 99% isopropyl alcohol to remove factory flux and dirt.
* **Identify exclusion zones**: Locate components that must *never* be coated. 
*

2. Protect Sensitive Components 

Mask off or strictly avoid the following components with your brush: 

* **Connectors & plugs**: Coating the pins inside pins/plugs will insulate them and break electrical connections.
* **USB ports**: Liquid inside the port will prevent data transfer and charging.
* **Barometers**: A barometer is a small metal square with a tiny hole; covering this hole ruins the altitude reading.
* **Boot buttons**: The mechanical switch will become glued shut and unusable. 
*

3. Apply the Coating 

* **First thin layer**: Dip a fine-tip nylon brush into the MG Chemicals 422B liquid. Apply a very thin, even layer across the PCB, working around the sensitive components. 
* **Cure time**: Wait **10 to 15 minutes** for the first layer to become tack-free before adding another. 
* **Second layer**: Apply a second coat perpendicular to the first layer to ensure total coverage and eliminate microscopic pinholes. 
* **Check coverage**: Use a UV blacklight if available; the MG Chemicals 422B formula contains a UV indicator that glows, highlighting any spots you missed. `[1][2][3]`
* **Final cure**: Let the electronics dry completely for **24 hours** before plugging in a battery or flying. 
*

4. Extra Protection for Payload Drones 

* **Capacitor legs**: Pay extra attention to the exposed legs of your main capacitor, as these carry high voltage and short easily in water.
* **Solder joints**: Ensure all heavy XT60 power leads and motor solder pads are fully encapsulated by the silicone layer. 

---

✅ Conformal Coating Complete 

Your FPV drone electronics are now insulated against water payloads. Ensure the coating has cured for a full 24 hours at room temperature before exposing the aircraft to moisture or conducting test flights.
