Integrating custom-built water gun drones into the **Senior Assassins** graduation game presents severe legal, safety, and regulatory challenges under Canadian law. 

Using a drone equipped with a motorized water payload significantly alters the traditional mechanics of the game and intersects directly with federal aviation laws and criminal statutes. 

Core Game Rules & Mechanics 

The game relies on tracking and elimination mechanics managed through community-driven platforms: 

* **Elimination:** Players must stalk and spray their assigned target with water.
* **Verification:** Targets must be tracked, and the "kill" must be caught on video proof and uploaded to trackers like the Splashin App.
* **Immunity:** Players gain temporary safety by wearing specific items, usually safety goggles or pool floaties.
* **Purge Days:** Organizers occasionally declare "Purge" periods where all immunity gear becomes invalid. 

Transport Canada Sub-250g Drone Regulations `[7][8][9][10][11][12]`

While micro-drones (weighing under 250 grams) do not require a pilot certificate or registration, they are **strictly regulated** under [Transport Canada CAR 901.02](https://tc.canada.ca/en/aviation/drone-safety/drone-pilot-licensing/get-permission-special-drone-operations/flying-your-drone-advertised-event) guidelines regarding reckless operation: 

* **No Advertised Events:** As of April 1, 2025, Transport Canada explicitly requires a **Special Flight Operations Certificate (SFOC)** to fly micro-drones under 250g at any advertised or community events. Operating without one carries individual fines up to **$1,000**. 
* **No Dropping or Spraying Payloads:** Federal aviation law prohibits operating a drone in a manner that creates a hazard to persons or property. Rigging a servo-controlled water pump or pressurized valve to spray a payload from mid-air is highly likely to be interpreted by authorities as a hazard or a modified flight risk. 
* **Line of Sight:** You must maintain a direct, unassisted visual line of sight with the drone at all times. `[1][2][3][4][5][6]`

Campus Operation & "Safe Zones" 

School properties and official campuses are universally designated as **Safe Zones** in Senior Assassins rules. 

* **Strict Prohibitions:** Traditional game rules completely ban gameplay inside school buildings, parking lots, and during sporting events. 
* **Administrative Penalties:** Violating these rules on campus results in immediate game disqualification and school suspension. Some high school administrations disqualify students from prom or graduation ceremonies for participating on school grounds. 
* **Lockdowns:** Canadian police forces, including the [Manitoba RCMP](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8-W1HvoGW1Y) and [York Regional Police](https://www.yrp.ca/news/police-warn-residents-senior-assassin-game-and-dangers-replica-firearms), have frequently placed schools on high-security lockdowns due to "weapons calls" generated by students playing Senior Assassins. 

Canadian Legal Considerations 

Operating a drone-mounted weapon in public risks serious criminal charges under the *Criminal Code of Canada*: 

* **Assault with a Weapon:** Sault Ste. Marie Police and other Ontario detachments have warned that spraying an individual without their consent can lead to criminal charges of assault. Using a mechanical device (the drone) to deliver the payload qualifies as utilizing an instrument to commit the act. 
* **Mischief and Disturbing the Peace:** Operating a drone low over public spaces or tracking individuals to spray them constitutes public mischief or criminal harassment. 
* **Replica Firearm Hazards:** Law enforcement must treat all weapons calls as active threats. Drones carrying custom payloads can be mistaken by bystanders as carrying explosive or dangerous devices, prompting a high-risk tactical police response. 
* **Civil Trespassing:** Hiding on, flying over, or creeping onto private residential property to stage a drone strike breaks provincial trespassing acts.
