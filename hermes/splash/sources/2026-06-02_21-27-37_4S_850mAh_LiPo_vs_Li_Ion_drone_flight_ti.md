For a **3.5-inch sub-250g drone**, the **4S 850mAh LiPo (or LiHV) is the definitive best battery choice**. A standard 4S Li-Ion pack made from cylindrical cells (like 18650s) is far too heavy, weighing roughly 200g on its own. This immediately pushes a 3.5-inch drone (which typically has a "dry" weight of 140g to 170g) well beyond the legal 250g threshold.

Critical Trade-offs Analyzed 

1. The Weight Penalty 

To stay under 250g, your battery allocation must not exceed **75g to 100g max** (assuming a 150g dry quad weight). 

* A **4S 850mAh LiPo** stays perfectly within this boundary at roughly 98g.
* **Lithium-Ion** cells are packaged in rigid steel cylinders (e.g., 18650 or 21700 formats). A single 18650 cell weighs roughly 48g. A 4S1P Li-Ion pack requires four cells, equating to roughly **192g** before adding wires, shrink wrap, and solder. It is physically impossible to build a sub-250g 3.5-inch drone with a traditional 4S Li-Ion pack. 

2. Power Delivery (C-Rating) vs. Motor Load 

A 3.5-inch quad demands rapid bursts of electricity, pulling upwards of 25A–40A during hard maneuvers. 

* **LiPo batteries** easily provide continuous discharge rates of 60C to 120C, maintaining strong voltage stability under heavy load.
* **Li-Ion packs** feature low discharge limits (typically 10A–15A continuous maximum). Pushing a Li-Ion pack with 3.5-inch propellers results in severe, immediate **voltage sag**, causing the drone to feel sluggish, drop out of the air during punch-outs, or trigger premature low-battery warnings.
