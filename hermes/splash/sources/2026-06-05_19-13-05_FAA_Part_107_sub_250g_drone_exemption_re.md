**Recreational drone pilots flying sub-250g drones cannot fly over people under any circumstances**, and dropping a payload like water triggers immediate federal violations. 

The Sub-250g Recreational Illusion `[1][2][3][4][5][6]`

*

* **Registration Exemption:** If your drone weighs under 250 grams (0.55 lbs) and you fly **strictly for leisure**, you are exempt from [FAA registration](https://www.faa.gov/uas/recreational_flyers) and Remote ID broadcast mandates.

* **Safety Rules Still Apply:** You must still pass the free online TRUST safety test and fully comply with recreational flying laws. 

*

Flight Over People Rules 

*

* **Recreational Bans:** The FAA strictly prohibits hobbyists from flying over human beings or moving vehicles, regardless of how light the drone is. 

* **Part 107 Limitation:** Only certified commercial Part 107 pilots are eligible for "Operations Over Human Beings" frameworks. 

* **The Weight Catch:** Even under Part 107 Category 1 rules (sub-250g), the drone cannot have exposed rotating parts that could lacerate skin. Adding required prop guards will push your sub-250g drone over the 250g limit, voiding your weight exemption and demanding individual aircraft registration. 

*

Dropping Payloads (Water) 

*

* **Hazardous Dropping Ban:** Federal regulation **§ 107.23 / Recreational Safe Operations** dictates that you cannot drop anything from an unmanned aircraft if it creates an undue hazard to persons or property. 

* **The Weight Shifter:** Attaching a mechanism to carry and drop water immediately adds to the "takeoff weight" of the drone. If the combined weight of your drone, the dropper, and the water exceeds 249 grams, your sub-250g registration exemption is instantly voided. You must then legally register the drone before taking off.
