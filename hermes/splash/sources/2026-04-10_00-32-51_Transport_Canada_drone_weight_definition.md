Under current **2026 Transport Canada** regulations, "operating weight" is defined as the weight of a remotely piloted aircraft at **any point during a flight**. This total includes: `[16][17][18]`

* **The aircraft itself** (frame and motors).
* **Any payload** (cameras, sensors, or slung loads).
* **Batteries** (including high-capacity versions that may push a drone over a weight threshold).
* **Safety equipment** (such as parachutes or safety cages). `[13][14][15]` **The remote control is not included** in this weight calculation. `[10][11][12]`

2026 Drone Weight Classifications 

Transport Canada uses these operating weights to determine which rules you must follow: `[7][8][9]`

* **Micro Drones (Less than 250 g):** These are exempt from registration and pilot certification but must be operated in a safe, non-reckless manner.
* **Small Drones (250 g to 25 kg):** Require [Drone Registration](https://tc.canada.ca/en/aviation/drone-safety/registering-your-drone) and at least a [Pilot Certificate – Basic Operations](https://tc.canada.ca/en/aviation/drone-safety/learn-rules-you-fly-your-drone/drone-operation-categories-pilot-certificates/basic-operations).
* **Medium Drones (25 kg to 150 kg):** A category fully implemented in late 2025; these typically require an Advanced Pilot Certificate and adhere to new technical standards for visual line-of-sight (VLOS) operations.
* **Large Drones (Over 150 kg):** Require a Special Flight Operations Certificate (SFOC). `[4][5][6]` **Critical Reminder:** If adding an accessory (like a propeller guard or larger battery) pushes your "micro drone" from 249 g to 250 g, it is legally reclassified as a **small drone** and must be registered immediately. `[1][2][3]`
