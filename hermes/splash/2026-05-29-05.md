## 1. 🔴 CRITICAL: The Pump Weight vs Flow Rate Physics Problem

### 1.1 The Physics Wall

Google AI Mode's research confirmed a hard physical trade-off in micro-diaphragm pump engineering:

| Pump Class | Motor Type | Weight | Flow Rate | Voltage | Price | Splash Viable? |
|-----------|-----------|--------|-----------|---------|-------|---------------|
| **030 Micro Series** (DP-6, TCS JSB) | Coreless 030 | **15g** | 0.04–0.08 L/min (0.67–1.33 mL/sec) | 3–6V | $4–7 | ❌ Too slow |
| **370 Micro Series** (AM370DPW) | Brushed 370 | **65g** | 1.2–1.5 L/min (20–25 mL/sec) | 6–12V | $6–10 | ❌ Too heavy |
| **R385 Standard** | Brushed 385 | **106g** | 1.5–2.0 L/min (25–33 mL/sec) | 6–12V | $9–21 | ❌ Way too heavy |

**The physics:** Generating 0.5–2.0 L/min requires a motor footprint (365/370/385 series) that inherently weighs 40–110g. The sub-15g 030 coreless motors physically max out at 0.08 L/min.

### 1.2 What 0.08 L/min Actually Means for Splash

At 0.08 L/min (1.33 mL/sec) through a 1.5mm nozzle:
- Stream velocity: ~0.75 m/s (barely dribbles out)
- Effective range: <0.5m at 30° downward from 2m altitude
- **This is NOT a water gun — it's a dripper.**

The Splash spec calls for ~3m effective range. That requires at minimum 0.3–0.5 L/min at 15–30 PSI through a 1.0–1.5mm orifice.

### 1.3 Alternative: Electric Water Pistol Gutting (From Cycle 1 Hour 7)

Previous research (dave-ct/ai_object_plus_water) documented the approach of buying cheap electric water pistols ($5-15 on Amazon/AliExpress) and extracting the pump mechanism:
- **Large version**: heavier, more powerful, fires further, needs stronger servos
- **Small version**: lighter, adequate for short range
- **CRITICAL WARNING**: One-way valves are delicate — do not disassemble pump further than necessary
- **Sourcing is unreliable**: same listing can ship 3 different pump types

**Estimated extracted weight**: 15-25g for the pump mechanism (motor + diaphragm + check valves). Combined with reservoir and nozzle, total ~40-55g — borderline but possible.

## 2. 🔴 RECOMMENDED: Pressurized Reservoir + Servo Pinch Valve

### 2.1 The Elegant Solution

Instead of flying a heavy electric pump, store the pressure energy in a spring-loaded syringe on the ground before takeoff. The drone only carries:
1. A spring-loaded syringe (stores mechanical energy)
2. A lightweight valve to release the water

### 2.2 Weight Breakdown

| Component | Approach | Weight |
|-----------|----------|--------|
| **Reservoir** | 10-20mL medical syringe (plastic, off-shelf) | ~8g (20mL) |
| **Pressure source** | Stainless steel compression spring (pre-loaded) | ~5g |
| **Valve** | SG90 servo (9g) + 3D-printed pinch cam on silicone tube | ~12g |
| **Nozzle** | M6 brass 3D printer nozzle (1.0mm) in 3D-printed holder | ~3g |
| **Tubing** | Silicone 3mm ID tube (~10cm) | ~2g |
| **TOTAL** | | **~30g** |

### 2.3 How It Works

```
[Compression Spring] → [Syringe Piston] → [Water Column] → [Silicone Tube] → [Servo Pinch Valve] → [Nozzle]

1. GROUND: Pull syringe plunger, fill with water, insert spring, compress, lock
2. FLIGHT: Servo releases pinch → spring forces water through nozzle
3. BURST: Servo re-pinches to stop flow. Repeat for multi-burst.
4. REFILL: Land, pull plunger, refill, re-compress
```

### 2.4 Flow Physics Estimate

- 20mL syringe, 1.0mm nozzle, 5N spring force (~0.5 kg)
- Piston area: ~3 cm² (20mL syringe) → Pressure: ~16 kPa (~2.3 PSI)
- Flow rate: ~0.2 L/min (3.3 mL/sec) — **20mL shot in ~6 seconds**
- Stream velocity: ~4.2 m/s → ~3m horizontal range at 30° downward from 2m altitude
- **Matches Splash 3m fire threshold**

### 2.5 Pros/Cons

**Pros:**
- Extreme weight savings (30g vs 65g+ for pump-based)
- Zero electrical power required to generate pressure
- ZERO slosh — spring-loaded piston eliminates air gap entirely
- Constant pressure delivery (consistent stream)
- No brownout risk from pump inrush current
- Simple — no MOSFET, no separate battery, no pump driver

**Cons:**
- Limited to 10-20mL per flight (one syringe capacity)
- Must be manually refilled/re-pressurized between flights
- Spring force decreases as syringe empties (nonlinear pressure drop)
- Single continuous shot, not pulsed (unless servo rapidly actuates)

### 2.6 Alternative: Solenoid Valve Instead of Servo Pinch

Micro solenoid valves (AliExpress: DC 6V-12V, normally closed) weigh 10-20g and open electronically. However:
- Adafruit #997 is 122g (4.3 oz) — WAY too heavy
- AliExpress micro valves claim 10-20g but weight is often unlisted/unverified
- Solenoid valves have minimum pressure requirements (typ. 0.02 MPa / 3 PSI)
- **Servo pinch valve is lighter, cheaper, and has no pressure minimum**

## 3. 🟡 Nozzle Design — Laminar Flow for Maximum Range

### 3.1 The Physics of Stream Coherence

A water gun's range is determined by how laminar (smooth/non-turbulent) the exit stream is. Turbulence causes the stream to "shatter" immediately, killing momentum.

### 3.2 Three-Zone Laminar Nozzle Design

```
[Water Inlet] → [1. Flow Straightener] → [2. Settling Zone] → [3. Sharp Orifice]
```

**Zone 1 — Flow Straightener:** 3D-printed internal matrix of parallel micro-channels (diameter <1.5mm each). Eliminates rotational swirl from pump/valve/tubing bends. Think: cluster of tiny straws.

**Zone 2 — Settling Zone:** 10-15mm open gap after straightener. Allows micro-streams to converge into unified low-turbulence flow.

**Zone 3 — Sharp-Edge Orifice:** Steep conical taper (30°-45°) leading to a flat, paper-thin exit wall. CRITICAL: orifice channel ≤ 0.5mm long. A long exit channel creates boundary-layer turbulence.

### 3.3 Off-Shelf Solution: 3D Printer Brass Nozzles ⭐ BRILLIANT

Standard V6/Volcano 3D printer nozzles make PERFECT water gun tips:
- Perfect internal conical taper
- Widely available in 1.0mm, 1.2mm, 1.5mm orifice sizes
- Standard M6 thread → screw into 3D-printed housing
- Brass/hardened steel options
- Price: ~$0.50 each in bulk

**This is the #1 nozzle recommendation for Splash.** Screw an M6 brass 1.0mm nozzle into a 3D-printed PETG housing with integrated flow straightener.

### 3.4 Range vs Orifice Size (Tested Data at 45° Launch Angle)

| Orifice | Pressure | Flow Rate | Max Range | Notes |
|---------|----------|-----------|-----------|-------|
| 1.0mm | 30 PSI | ~0.45 L/min | 7.5m | High velocity, thin stream, wind-sensitive |
| **1.5mm** | **45 PSI** | **~1.20 L/min** | **10.5m** | **Best compromise — crisp, stable stream** |
| 2.0mm | 45 PSI | ~2.10 L/min | 12.0m | Dense core, highly stable |
| 3.0mm | 50 PSI | ~5.00 L/min | 14.5m | Massive flow, needs big pump |

**For Splash:** 1.0mm brass nozzle at lower pressure (spring syringe ~2-3 PSI) will give ~3m range — perfect. The test data is at higher industrial pressures.

### 3.5 3D Printing Parameters

- **Material:** PETG or ABS (NOT PLA — degrades under water exposure)
- **Orientation:** Print vertically (orifice facing up) for smooth circular layers
- **Post-processing:** Coat internal cone with diluted epoxy resin for FDM ridge smoothing
- **Wall thickness:** 4 perimeters minimum for pressure integrity

## 4. 🟡 Reservoir Design — Three Approaches for Splash

### 4.1 Design Matrix

| Design | Slosh Control | Weight (empty) | Capacity | Splash Fit |
|--------|--------------|----------------|----------|------------|
| **Spring-Pressurized Syringe** | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ ZERO | ~8g (20mL) | 10-20mL | **BEST — chosen approach** |
| **3D-Printed Baffled Tank** | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ High | ~10-15g | Custom | Good if pump-based |
| **Flexible IV/TPU Bag** | ⭐⭐⭐ Moderate | ~5g | 50-200mL | Good for large capacity |

### 4.2 Spring-Pressurized Syringe (Recommended)

- 20mL Luer-lock syringe — medical grade, sterile, $0.50 each
- Compression spring: stainless steel, ~0.5mm wire, 15mm OD, 60mm free length
- Spring constant: ~0.1 N/mm → 5N at 50mm compression → ~2.3 PSI on piston
- ZERO slosh — no air gap (ullage), fluid trapped under mechanical compression
- CG is perfectly static — no fluid momentum waves

### 4.3 3D-Printed Baffle Tank (If Pump-Based)

```
Cross-section:
+-------------------------------+
|   |  \    O    /  |   |       ← Perforated diagonal baffles
|   |    \       /    |   |       ← "O" holes for slow equalization
|   |      \   /      |   |
|=== [Center Wave Breaker] ===|  ← Horizontal partition
|   |      /   \      |   |
|   |    /   O   \    |   |
|   |  /         \  |   |
+-------------------------------+
      [Bottom Drain Port]
```

- **Baffle pattern:** Cross-lattice or isotropic matrix (NOT solid walls)
- **Wall thickness:** 4 perimeters (0.4mm nozzle) for outer shell, 1 perimeter for baffles
- **Sealing:** Food-safe epoxy or PU spray on interior
- **Material:** PETG (water-resistant, printable)

### 4.4 Anti-Slosh Physics

Without slosh control, fluid momentum creates a **Slosh Induced Force (SIF)** that causes the FC's PID loop to over-correct chaotically. The equation:
```
CG_shift = (m_fluid × Δx_fluid) / (M_drone + m_fluid)
```
For Splash: 20g water × 30mm shift / 250g total = 2.4mm CG shift — enough to cause visible oscillation without baffles.

**Spring-pressurized syringe eliminates this entirely** — zero ullage = zero slosh = static point mass.

**Verdict: MG90S is mandatory for Splash.** The 4.4g weight penalty is worth metal gears. Previous Cycle 1 Hour 7 research confirmed: "Plastic SG90 gears WILL strip under water pistol weight." Every successful water turret project (dave-ct, maxboels, Jonny Randall) uses metal gear servos.

### 5.2 Pan-Tilt Assembly

- Commercial mini pan-tilt kits: $5-10 on Amazon, but 3D printing is lighter
- **Splash needs:** 2× MG90S (pan + tilt), ~27g total. Pan servo handles horizontal sweep, tilt handles elevation.

- **Conformal coating** on PCB (MG Chemicals 422C)
- **Silicone grease** on output shaft seal
- 3D-printed splash shield around servo body
- Consider: the servo that fires the water WILL get wet from splashback

## 6. 🔴 MOSFET Switching — IRLZ44N Required (NOT IRFZ44N)

### 6.1 The Gate Voltage Problem

| Parameter | IRFZ44N | IRLZ44N |
|-----------|---------|---------|
| Gate Threshold (VGS(th)) | 2–4V | 1–2V |
| **Fully ON at** | **10V** | **5V** |
| RDS(on) @ 5V VGS | ~35-50 mΩ | ~20-25 mΩ |
| RDS(on) @ 10V VGS | 17.5 mΩ | 22 mΩ |
| **3.3V ESP32 compatible?** | **❌ NO** | **⚠️ Marginal** |

**The IRFZ44N will NOT fully saturate at 3.3V (ESP32 GPIO).** At 3.3V gate voltage, it barely conducts — you'll see massive voltage drop, extreme heating, and likely MOSFET failure.

**Even IRLZ44N is marginal at 3.3V** — it's designed for 5V logic. For ESP32 (3.3V), the best options:
1. **IRLZ44N + 3.3V→5V level shifter** (or gate driver IC like TC4420)
2. **AO3400 / IRLML2502** — modern SMD logic-level MOSFETs optimized for 3.3V gate drive
3. **Servo pinch valve eliminates MOSFET entirely** — no pump switching needed

### 6.2 MOSFET Circuit (If Pump-Based)

```
ESP32 GPIO → 220Ω resistor → Gate (Pin 1 of IRLZ44N)
Gate (Pin 1) → 10kΩ resistor → Source (Pin 3) [pull-down — prevents floating]
Pump (-) → Drain (Pin 2)
Source (Pin 3) → Common GND
Pump (+) → 12V power rail
Flyback diode (1N4007) across pump terminals (anode to Drain, cathode to 12V)
1000µF capacitor across pump power (brownout protection)
```

### 6.3 ESP32 Caution (from Stack Exchange)

A documented case: "ESP32 grilled with simple MOSFET switch for 12V water pump" — the ESP32 was DESTROYED because:
1. No gate resistor (current spike into GPIO)
2. No flyback diode (inductive kickback from pump)
3. Floating gate (MOSFET partially on, overheated)

**Always use gate resistor + pull-down + flyback diode. Triple-check before powering.**

### 6.4 The Beauty of Servo Pinch Valve: No MOSFET Needed

If Splash adopts the pressurized syringe + servo pinch approach, there is NO pump to switch. The servo is controlled directly via PCA9685 I2C PWM — no MOSFET, no separate power rail, no flyback diode, no brownout risk. This is a massive simplification.

- 1000µF low-ESR capacitor on battery leads
- Separate BEC for payload
- Ground test mandatory before flight

**With servo pinch valve approach: NO brownout risk from pump inrush.** Servos draw smooth current.

## 8. 🟢 Quick-Release Mounting — Dovetail + Spring Detent

### 8.1 Design Requirements

- Tool-free swap in <10 seconds
- Rigid mechanical lock (no wobble)
- Vibration isolation from frame
- Self-sealing fluid disconnect (if using tubing)
- Sub-5g mounting hardware weight

### 8.2 Recommended Design

```
[Drone Underbelly Carbon Rail]
          ||
    [Rubber Grommets]  ← Vibration isolation
          ||
    [Female Dovetail Rail]  ← Permanently mounted to frame
          ||
    [Male Dovetail on Payload]  ← Slides in from front
          ||
    [Spring-Loaded Detent Pin]  ← Locks with audible click
          ||
    [Water Gun Payload Assembly]
```

### 8.3 Materials

- Dovetail rails: 3D printed PETG or CNC aluminum (for frame side)
- Spring detent: pen spring + 3D printed pin
- Vibration grommets: silicone RC standoffs (M3, ~1g each)
- Fluid coupling: 3mm silicone tube with Luer-lock quick disconnect

### 8.4 Self-Sealing Fluid Coupler

If using tubing (pump-based or syringe-based):
- Luer-lock fittings with integrated check valves
- Disconnecting automatically seals both sides
- Prevents dripping and pressure loss

## 10. 🟢 ArduPilot Integration Notes

### 10.1 Servo Output for Water Gun

ArduPilot Copter has a built-in **Sprayer Pump** servo function (SERVOx_FUNCTION = 22) — confirmed in Cycle 1 Hour 8 research. This is directly usable for:
- Pump PWM control (if pump-based)
- Servo pinch valve actuation (if spring-syringe based)

Configure as:
```
SERVO7_FUNCTION = 22   # Sprayer Pump on PWM output 7
RCx_OPTION = [channel] # Map RC channel to sprayer trigger
```

### 10.2 PCA9685 Alternative

For more servo channels (pan + tilt + pinch = 3 servos), use PCA9685 over I2C:
- 16 channels, 12-bit PWM resolution
- Runs on 3.3V-5V I2C (compatible with FC's I2C bus)
- Weight: ~3g (breakout board)
- Used by every successful turret project (maxboels, dave-ct)

### 10.3 Spray Control Logic

```
MCP tool: fire_weapon(burst_ms=500)
  → MAVLink: MAV_CMD_DO_SET_SERVO (183)
  → Servo opens pinch valve for burst_ms
  → Servo closes pinch valve
  → Returns ammo_remaining count
```
