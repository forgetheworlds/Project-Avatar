## §2. 🟢 MG90S vs SG90 — Definitive Servo Selection

**Source:** Google AI Mode (24 sources, servo datasheets, user reviews)

### Head-to-Head

| Spec | SG90 | MG90S |
|---|---|---|
| **Weight** | 9g | **13g** (+4g) |
| **Gears** | Nylon/Plastic ❌ | **Metal** (brass + aluminum) ✅ |
| **Torque (4.8V)** | 1.8 kg·cm | **1.8 kg·cm** (same) |
| **Torque (6.0V)** | Not recommended ❌ | **2.2 kg·cm** ✅ |
| **Speed (4.8V)** | 0.12 s/60° | **0.10 s/60°** ✅ |
| **Speed (6.0V)** | N/A | **0.08 s/60°** ✅ |
| **Bearing** | Plastic bushing ❌ | **Top ball bearing** ✅ |
| **Continuous Pan** | Low — gears strip | **High — metal gears handle it** |
| **Price** | ~$3 | ~$5-7 |

### Why MG90S Wins for Splash

1. **Recoil handling**: Water pump recoil (~0.5-1.7N) creates a torque moment on the pan servo. SG90's 1.8 kg·cm at 4.8V with plastic gears WILL strip under repeated recoil. MG90S at 6V (2.2 kg·cm) with metal gears handles it.
2. **Continuous pan tracking**: Plastic bushing on SG90 ovalizes under continuous panning → loose, shaky gimbal. MG90S ball bearing maintains precision.
3. **Speed for tracking**: 0.08 s/60° at 6V vs 0.12 s/60° — 33% faster response for moving target tracking.
4. **Water resistance**: Metal gears less affected by corrosion than plastic degradation. Still needs conformal coating.

**Recommendation: MG90S for BOTH pan and tilt axes. Run at 6V via separate BEC.** The 4g weight penalty per servo (8g total for 2-axis) is negligible compared to the reliability improvement.

**Power note:** MG90S at 6V draws 250-700mA while moving, ~5-10mA idle. A 5V/3A BEC can handle both servos + ESP32.

## §3. 🟢 Nozzle Ballistics & Fluid Dynamics

**Source:** Google AI Mode (rocket nozzle design references, fluid dynamics)

### Optimal Nozzle Geometry
- **Bore profile**: Smooth conical taper (12° half-angle) → straight cylindrical exit land
- **Orifice diameter**: 2.0mm (balance between range and flow rate)
- **Land length**: 2-3× orifice diameter (4-6mm for 2mm orifice)
- **Material**: PETG (not PLA — better interlayer bonding, hydrostatic burst pressure)
- **Print orientation**: Vertical (orifice up) — eliminates internal supports, keeps bore concentric
- **Slicing**: 100% concentric infill at nozzle tip, 5+ perimeter walls

### Ballistics Derivation

**Exit velocity** (Bernoulli):
```
V = Cd × √(2P/ρ)
```
Where Cd = 0.95 (smooth conical nozzle), P = pump pressure (Pa), ρ = 1000 kg/m³

**At 3 bar (385 pump max):**
V = 0.95 × √(2 × 300,000 / 1000) = 0.95 × √600 ≈ **23.27 m/s**

**At 0.5 bar (370 pump realistic):**
V = 0.95 × √(2 × 50,000 / 1000) = 0.95 × √100 ≈ **9.5 m/s**

**Volumetric flow rate** (2mm orifice, 3 bar):
A = π(0.002)²/4 = 3.142 × 10⁻⁶ m²
Q = A × V = 3.142 × 10⁻⁶ × 23.27 = **4.39 L/min** (far exceeds pump capability)

**Recoil force:**
F = ρ × Q × V = 1000 × (7.31 × 10⁻⁵) × 23.27 ≈ **1.70N (173g force)**

**At realistic 370 pump (0.5 bar, 0.8 L/min):**
V ≈ 9.5 m/s, Q ≈ 1.33 × 10⁻⁵ m³/s (0.8 L/min)
F ≈ 1000 × 1.33 × 10⁻⁵ × 9.5 ≈ **0.126N (12.8g force)** — manageable!

**Maximum trajectory range:**
R = V² × sin(2θ) / g × η_drag

At 3 bar: R ≈ 23.27² × sin(70°) / 9.81 × 0.25 ≈ **11.8m** (theoretical)
At 0.5 bar: R ≈ 9.5² × sin(70°) / 9.81 × 0.25 ≈ **2.2m** (theoretical, ~1.5m actual with drag)

### Practical Range Estimate for Splash

| Pump | Pressure | Exit Velocity | Theoretical Range | Effective Range |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 370 diaphragm | 0.3-0.5 bar | 7-9.5 m/s | 1.5-2.2m | **1.0-1.5m** |
| 385 diaphragm | 1-2 bar | 13-19 m/s | 4-8m | **2-4m** |
| Coreless gear | 0.2-0.4 bar | 6-8 m/s | 1-1.8m | **0.8-1.2m** |

**KEY INSIGHT:** To achieve 3m effective range, you need ~1.5-2 bar pump pressure. Only the 385 pump can deliver this — but at 110g weight penalty. The 370 pump at 0.5 bar max will only achieve ~1.5m effective range.

### Nozzle Fabrication Directives
1. Print PETG vertically with exit orifice pointing up
2. 100% concentric infill at tip + 5 perimeter walls minimum
3. Post-process: run sharp 2mm drill bit through exit land manually to clear stringing
4. Install inline 50-100 mesh filter BEFORE nozzle to prevent clogging (from F-2026-06-05-073)
5. Add check valve to prevent siphoning/dripping when pump is off

## §4. 🟢 Reservoir Design — Flexible Bag Wins

**Source:** Google AI Mode (medical IV bag specifications, TPU bladder designs)

### Tank Type Comparison

| Feature | Flexible Bag (TPU/IV) | Rigid Tank (3D Printed) | Syringe (Polypropylene) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Dry Weight** | **1-2g** (lowest) | 4-6g | 6-9g (heaviest) |
| **Slosh Control** | **Perfect** (collapses) | Poor (needs baffles) | Perfect (plunger seals) |
| **CG Stability** | **Excellent** (sym collapse) | Poor (fluid shifts) | Moderate (linear shift) |
| **Priming** | Needs pump suction | Needs air vent | Self-pressurizing |
| **Cost** | ~$1 (salvaged IV bag) | ~$0.50 (printed) | ~$1-2 |
| **Durability** | Moderate (puncture risk) | High | High |

**Winner: Flexible IV bag or TPU bladder — 1-2g dry weight, zero slosh, perfect CG.**

### Mounting Design
- **Location**: Centered directly under drone's vertical CG on bottom plate
- **Orientation**: Flattened horizontally, exit port facing rearward toward pump
- **Securing**: Soft Velcro strap (adds ~0.5g) — allows quick swap
- **Quick Release**: Magnetic slide rail (3×1mm Neodymium magnets) + Luer-lock quick-disconnect with integrated check valve

### Anti-Sloshing Physics
In a rigid 20mL tank that's half-empty, 10g of water slams to one side during sudden pitch/roll. For a ~250g drone, this creates a destabilizing moment the flight controller struggles to counteract. A flexible TPU bag has ZERO free-surface effect — it collapses under atmospheric pressure as water is drawn out, keeping remaining fluid perfectly centered.

### Capacity Decision
- **15mL reservoir** = ~15g water + 1-2g bag = ~17g payload (water)
- At 0.8 L/min (370 pump): 15mL empties in ~1.1 seconds of continuous fire
- At 0.5 L/min: ~1.8 seconds
- **Recommendation**: Multiple short bursts (0.2-0.5s each) for 3-9 shots per fill
- For protection mode: 15mL allows ~3-5 engagements before reload

## §5. 🔴 MOSFET Pump Trigger Circuit — Critical Correction

**Source:** Google AI Mode (IRFZ44N datasheet, logic-level MOSFET comparison, wiring diagrams)

### The Problem
The IRFZ44N is a **standard N-channel MOSFET** that needs Vgs ≥ 10V to fully saturate (lowest Rds(on)). Driving it with 3.3V or 5V FC GPIO pins forces it into the **linear/active region** — causing massive heat dissipation and potential failure at pump currents >2-3A.

### The Fix: Use Logic-Level MOSFET
- **IRL3705N** or **IRLZ44N** (the "L" stands for logic-level)
- These achieve full saturation at Vgs = 3.3-5V
- Same pinout as IRFZ44N, similar current rating, lower gate threshold

### Correct Circuit Diagram

```
[FC GPIO] → [220Ω resistor] → Gate (G) of IRLZ44N
                               10kΩ pull-down resistor (Gate → Source/GND)
                               
[2S LiPo +] ─┬─→ [BEC 5V/3A] ──→ Servo (+) 
             │                                     [1N4007] (cathode→+)
             └─→ [PUMP (+)] ────────────────────────┤
                                                     │
[GND] ──→ [BEC GND] ──→ Servo (-) ──→ FC GND        │
           ↓                                         │
      Source (S) of IRLZ44N ◄──────────────────── [PUMP (-)]
           ↑
      10kΩ ↓ GND
      Gate ──→ 220Ω ──→ FC GPIO
```

### Critical Wiring Rules
1. **Common ground**: Battery negative, BEC ground, FC ground, MOSFET source ALL must connect together
2. **Flyback diode**: 1N4007 directly across pump terminals (cathode/stripe to pump +)
3. **Gate resistor**: 220Ω in series with GPIO (limits inrush current to gate capacitance)
4. **Pull-down**: 10kΩ gate-to-source (prevents floating gate — pump stays OFF during FC boot)
5. **BEC**: Separate 5V/3A BEC for servo + FC logic (isolation from pump noise)

### Power Distribution Recommendation
- **Main battery**: 4S 850mAh → FC power (via onboard BEC) + motors
- **Payload power**: 2S 300mAh (separate) → serves as pump power source
- **Servo power**: 5V/3A BEC from 2S battery → powers MG90S servos at 6V
- **FC signal**: FC GPIO → 220Ω → MOSFET gate (controlling pump ground path)
- This avoids putting pump noise on FC power rail

**This corrects prior wiring assumptions** that used IRFZ44N directly. All prior FC-based pump switching designs need this update.

## §6. 🟢 Competitor Analysis — Build Classes & Lessons

**Source:** Google AI Mode (24 sources, Reddit, Hackaday, Instructables, GitHub)

### Three Build Classes Identified

| Class | AUW | Fluid | Range | Flight Time | Controller | Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Micro Wi-Fi Squirter** | 35-50g | 5-10mL | 0.5-1m | 3-4 min | ESP32 onboard | ESP-Fly kit |
| **Mid-Tier FPV Stracker** | 650-950g | 150-250mL | 2-4m | 4-6 min | Betaflight + ESP32 | 5-7" quad + Super Soaker |
| **Heavy-Lift Custom Lifter** | 1.8-2.8kg | 750-1000mL | 5-10m | 8-12 min | Pixhawk/ArduPilot | Octo + CPS bladder |

### Where Splash Fits
**Splash occupies a NOVEL GAP** — there is NO existing sub-250g drone with water GUN (projectile) capability. The closest is the "Micro Wi-Fi Squirter" class which is an aerial dropper (0.5-1m range), not a projectile. Splash would be the FIRST in this weight class with true aimed water projectile capability.

However, the closest functional match is the **Mid-Tier FPV Stracker** (650-950g) — scaled down to sub-250g. The key challenge is that existing sub-250g attempts (the ESP-Fly and similar) use tiny 3V centrifugal pumps that can't produce meaningful range.

### What Failed (12 documented failure modes)

| Failure | Cause | Mitigation for Splash |
|---|---|---|
| **CG shift from rigid tanks** | Water sloshing destabilizes drone → PID oscillation → crash | Use flexible IV bag/TPU bladder (collapses, no slosh) |
| **FC fried by pump back-EMF** | No flyback diode → voltage spikes >100V → MOSFET/FC destruction | 1N4007 directly across pump terminals (REQUIRED) |
| **Plastic servo gears stripped** | SG90 can't handle continuous pan + recoil | MG90S metal gears + ball bearing (upgrade confirmed) |
| **Nozzle clogging** | Unfiltered water clogs 1.5-2mm orifices | 50-100 mesh inline filter before nozzle |
| **Optical flow confusion** | Downward sensors confused by water/puddles | Use GPS + barometer for altitude; avoid optical flow near water |
| **Brownouts under pump load** | Pump + servos + FC sharing same regulator | Separate 2S battery for pump; separate BEC for servos |
| **Siphoning/dripping after fire** | Water continues flowing by gravity after pump stops | Check valve at nozzle + vacuum relief |
| **Water damage to electronics** | Aerosolized mist blows back into frame during flight | Conformal coating (MG 422B silicone) on ALL boards |
| **Pump burnout (dry running)** | Running pump with no water → diaphragm damage | Optical fluid sensor + dry-run cutoff in state machine |
| **Telemetry loss during engagement** | WiFi congestion or ESP32 overload | MAVLink over ELRS (separate from WiFi) + RTL failsafe |
| **Target loss during tracking** | YOLO drops detection momentarily | Kalman filter for smooth prediction through occlusions |
| **Prop wash destabilization** | Heavy payload + aggressive maneuvers | Gentle flight envelope; avoid rapid yaw during engagement |

### What Worked (Proven Solutions)
1. **Flexible bladders** (IV bags, silicone tubes) → zero slosh, stable CG
2. **Check valves** → no siphoning/dripping between shots
3. **Conformal coating** → mandatory for water-proximity electronics
4. **Separate power** for pump and flight systems → no brownouts
5. **Metal gear servos** → survive continuous pan-tilt operation
6. **Inline filters** → prevent nozzle clogging from debris
